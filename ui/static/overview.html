The problem

A failed payment is not automatically worth chasing.

Every recovery attempt spends something real: a message to a customer, a slot in that customer's limited attention, agent capacity, merchant discount budget, and a step down the curve of how likely they are to respond next time. Spend those on the wrong payment and you have made the next recovery harder and lost money doing it.

What a recovery attempt costs

  • A contact the customer will not welcome twice
  • Agent capacity that another opportunity needed
  • Merchant discount budget, once spent
  • Customer fatigue — the next attempt converts less
  • Regulatory exposure if timing or consent is wrong

So the real question is not "how do we retry?"

Is recovering this payment worth attempting right now?

Answering that needs something no per-request permission check can provide: a view of every agent's demand on the same customer, the budget they share, and what that contact costs the future.

SAMPARK is not picking the bigger payment. It compares each opportunity's expected net value against what spending this customer's one available contact costs the future. On this data that cost is about ₹541 per contact, which puts break-even for a failed payment near ₹1,978. Agents

Several agents want the same customer. Only one should get them.

A recovery agent is an automated worker with a narrow job — retry a failed payment, rescue an abandoned cart, chase an overdue invoice. Each one can hold valid consent, an approved scope, an in-policy discount and a registered template and still be the wrong one to act, because the cost of burning a customer falls on whoever needs them next week. Authorization answers one request at a time and cannot see that.

1

Identity

Every request is signed with the agent's own Ed25519 key and verified before anything else happens.

2

Scope

A declared capability — which channels, intents, risk sources and discount ceiling. Out of scope is refused without the allocator ever running.

3

Limits

Rate ceilings, contact caps, quiet hours and a shared merchant budget. Repeated protocol misuse accumulates strikes and revokes the key.

4

Allocation

Among the agents that may act, one is chosen on expected net value. The rest are deferred or declined, with a reason.

Why it could matter to Razorpay

Four things this layer adds.

Better recovery efficiency

Prioritise economically worthwhile interventions instead of treating every failed payment identically.

Less unnecessary contact

Avoid repeatedly contacting customers where the expected recovery value is negative.

Controlled agent automation

Third-party agents operate inside scopes, budgets, rate limits, quiet hours and revocation rules.

Auditable decisions

Every meaningful decision is a hash-chained event, replayable and independently verifiable.

These are properties of the decision layer as built and tested here. No revenue improvement is claimed or guaranteed — see the evidence below.

Evidence

What the experiment actually found.

Five precommitted seeds, one synthetic merchant month, four identical agents in both arms. These are the committed results, including the ones that do not flatter us.

−48.5 % fewer contacts
−8.2 % lower total revenue recovered
1.687× revenue per contact
0.00 % contribution from the ML models
SAMPARK recovered less total money, and we are not hiding it. It recovered that money with roughly half the contacts, which is the point: the system optimises recovery efficiency, not retry volume. If your objective is to maximise gross recovered rupees regardless of what it costs the customer relationship, this is the wrong layer.
The ML models did not improve on the heuristic. The uplift model is honestly unavailable on this data — there is no untreated control population to learn from — so its contribution is exactly zero. SAMPARK therefore rests on no unsupported ML claim. The model seam exists, is tested, and degrades to the frozen heuristic with a logged event.

A sensitivity sweep that argues against us

A 50-point sweep over the two ground-truth response coefficients — the grid, the metric and six predictions committed to git before the sweep ran. All six passed. It also found that selection and allocation, not the cross-agent fatigue term, explain most of the advantage: switch fatigue off entirely and about 73 % of the win survives. The specification called fatigue "the whole thesis expressed as arithmetic". The measurement disagrees, and that is reported here rather than left for a reviewer to find.

Compliance is the part that does not move

Across every committed run: zero quiet-hour violations, zero contact-cap breaches, zero interlock violations, and zero scope violations by the well-behaved agents. That last figure is the control — it is expected to read zero, and it does while every other number moves, which is the cleanest available evidence that authorization was never the binding constraint.

Honesty

What is real, what is simulated, and what is not.

These four categories are used consistently across every page of this product. Nothing is ever shown in a way that blurs them.

Not demonstrated Not shown here at all, and claimed nowhere. Live S2S payment initiation; live LLM policy compilation; any production deployment.

Honest limitations

  • The data is synthetic. 5,000 generated people, ~20,000 risk items, one simulated month. Reproducible and falsifiable — not evidence about the real world.
  • The break-even figure is a property of that model, not of any real merchant. The mechanism transfers; the number does not.
  • Channels are mocked on purpose. You cannot lawfully message real numbers on synthetic consent, so the payload that would have been sent is logged instead.
  • No load test, no p99 latency measurement. Reported as not measured rather than estimated.

What is genuinely demonstrated

  • A real Razorpay Test Mode integration, via the Razorpay MCP Server, with provenance that cannot be fabricated.
  • A complete decision path from a real failed payment to a bounded, recorded outcome.
  • A hash-chained audit log you can verify from the UI in one click.
  • Deterministic replay of the failure and recovery mechanisms at scale.
